# Dress Code Guide

## Casual

Casual means everyday clothes chosen for comfort: t-shirts, hoodies, sweaters,
jeans, joggers, sneakers, and casual jackets such as a denim or puffer jacket.
Casual is right for errands, friends, sport, and relaxed weekends.

## Smart casual

Smart casual sits between casual and business. It means neat, well-fitting
clothes without a suit or tie: a collared shirt such as an oxford shirt or a
fine knit sweater, chinos or dark well-kept jeans without rips, and leather
shoes such as loafers or Chelsea boots. A trench coat or a plain wool coat
works as outerwear. Avoid sportswear, hoodies, worn sneakers, and shorts.
Smart casual suits dinners at nicer restaurants, dates, and many offices.

## Business and formal

Business dress means a suit with a collared shirt, usually with a tie, and
polished leather shoes. Formal or black tie events need a tuxedo or a dark
suit. The wardrobe in this project has no suits, so business and formal
outfits cannot be built from it.

## Interviews

For a job interview, dress one level more formal than the people who already
work there. For a relaxed office that is usually smart casual: a collared shirt,
chinos, and leather shoes.

## Weddings and events

A wedding invitation normally states the dress code. If it does not, ask the
hosts, because it depends on the venue and the time of day. Avoid wearing white
to a wedding as a guest.
