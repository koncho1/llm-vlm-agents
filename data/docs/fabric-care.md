# Fabric Care Guide

## Wool sweaters and knitwear

Wash wool by hand in cool water (about 30 °C at most) with a wool or mild
detergent, or use the wool cycle of the washing machine. Do not wring or twist
the garment. Press the water out by rolling it in a towel, then dry it flat on
a towel away from radiators and direct sunlight. Hanging wet wool stretches it,
and hot water or tumble drying shrinks and felts it. Wool needs washing less
often than cotton: airing it out overnight is usually enough between wears.

## Pilling on knitwear

Pilling is the small fuzzy balls that form on knitted jumpers and sweaters
where the fabric rubs, for example under the arms or where a bag strap sits.
Remove the pills with a fabric shaver or a sweater comb, working gently on a
flat surface. Washing knitwear inside out and on a gentle cycle reduces new
pilling. Pilling is normal for wool and does not mean the garment is damaged.

## Cotton t-shirts and shirts

Cotton t-shirts and shirts can be machine washed at 30–40 °C. Turn printed or
dark items inside out to protect the colour. Cotton can shrink a little in a
hot tumble dryer, so dry it on low heat or on a hanger. Iron oxford shirts
while slightly damp for the best result.

## Denim jeans

Wash dark denim jeans rarely and inside out in cold water to keep the colour
from fading. Skip the tumble dryer and hang them to dry. Spot clean small marks
instead of washing the whole pair.

## Waterproof rain jackets

Clean a waterproof rain jacket with a technical wash, not normal detergent or
fabric softener, which clog the membrane. When water stops beading on the
surface, re-waterproof the jacket: wash it, then apply a spray-on or wash-in
DWR (durable water repellent) treatment, and finish with a short low-heat tumble
dry or iron on low heat if the care label allows it, which reactivates the
coating.

## Hoodies and sweatpants

Wash hoodies and jogger sweatpants inside out at 30 °C to limit pilling and
fading. Close zips before washing. Air drying keeps the fleece lining soft.
