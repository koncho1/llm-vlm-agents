# Layering and Weather Guide

## The three-layer system

Dress in layers so you can adjust to the temperature. A base layer next to the
skin (t-shirt or thin shirt), a mid layer for warmth (sweater, flannel shirt,
or hoodie), and an outer layer against wind and rain (rain jacket, puffer, or
coat). Remove or add the mid layer as the day warms or cools.

## Near-freezing and cold days

Around 0 °C and below, wear all three layers: a base layer, a warm mid layer
such as a wool sweater, and an insulated outer layer such as a puffer jacket.
Add a hat such as a wool beanie and a scarf, because a lot of comfort is lost
at the head and neck. Choose closed, waterproof boots over sneakers.

## Rainy days

For rain, the outer layer and the shoes matter most. Wear a waterproof rain
jacket or a trench coat and waterproof boots. Prefer jeans or dark trousers to
light-coloured fabrics, which show water marks. Carry an umbrella when the
wind is not too strong; in strong wind, a hooded rain jacket is more reliable
than an umbrella.

## Windy days

Wind makes it feel several degrees colder than the thermometer says. A
windproof outer layer such as a rain jacket or puffer blocks the wind, and a
scarf and beanie protect the neck and ears.

## Hot and sunny days

In heat, choose loose, breathable fabrics such as cotton and light colours that
reflect sunlight. Shorts and t-shirts are fine for casual plans. Wear a cap or
hat for sun protection and breathable shoes such as canvas sneakers.

## Mild days

On mild days of about 14–20 °C, a t-shirt or shirt with a light jacket such as
a denim jacket is usually enough. Keep a light layer with you in case the
evening cools down.
