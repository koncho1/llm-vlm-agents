# Shoe Care Guide

## Suede shoes and boots in wet weather

Suede is easily stained by water and salt. Avoid wearing suede shoes such as
suede Chelsea boots in rain, slush, or snow. If suede gets wet, blot it with a
dry cloth, let it dry naturally at room temperature away from heaters, and
brush the nap back up with a suede brush once dry. A suede protector spray
reduces water damage but does not make suede waterproof.

## Leather shoes and loafers

Clean smooth leather shoes with a damp cloth, then apply shoe cream or polish
every few weeks to keep the leather supple. Use shoe trees made of cedar to
keep the shape and absorb moisture. Leather loafers tolerate light drizzle, but
heavy rain can mark them and soak the sole.

## Waterproof boots

Waterproof boots are the best choice for rain, snow, and slush. Rinse off mud
and road salt after wearing them, since salt dries out leather and leaves white
marks. Let them dry at room temperature with the insoles removed. Reapply a
waterproofing wax or spray when water stops beading on the surface.

## Canvas sneakers and running shoes

Canvas sneakers and mesh running shoes soak through quickly and are not
suitable for rain. Clean canvas sneakers by hand with a soft brush and mild
soap; machine washing can damage the glue. Remove the laces and insoles before
cleaning and air dry them stuffed with paper.

## Rotating shoes

Avoid wearing the same pair two days in a row. Leather and the inside lining
need about 24 hours to dry out fully from foot moisture, and rotating pairs
makes shoes last noticeably longer.
